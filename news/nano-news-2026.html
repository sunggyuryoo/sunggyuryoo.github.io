Remote contact doping research featured by SNU

Improving charge injection at metal–semiconductor contacts is essential for high-performance two-dimensional phototransistors. However, conventional contact doping can introduce charged dopants in or near the channel, creating additional noise that limits photodetection.

We used optical programming to selectively dope the contacts of WSe2 phototransistors. We placed patterned hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) only beneath the contact regions and trapped charges using light and a gate voltage. The trapped charges improved injection through remote electrostatic doping while remaining spatially separated from the transport channel.

Following programming, contact resistance decreased, normalized low-frequency noise fell by approximately a factor of 100, and photoresponsivity increased by about a factor of ten. These results show that selective remote contact doping can strengthen the optical signal while suppressing noise, providing a route to more sensitive two-dimensional phototransistors.

Our study was published in ACS Nano and featured on the website of Seoul National University’s Department of Physics and Astronomy.
